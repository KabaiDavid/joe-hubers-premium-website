## The business

- **Joe Huber's Family Farm & Restaurant**, 2421 Engle Rd., Starlight, IN 47106.
  Main line (812) 923-5255 (reservations by phone). info@joehubers.com.
  22 miles north of downtown Louisville, KY.
- **Heritage**: Simon Huber came from Baden-Baden, Germany, in 1843. Joseph Sr. & Mary
  bought the farm in 1926 (11 children; Mary cooked for the farm hands, and her chicken
  and dumplings were the favourite). In 1967 Joe Jr. & Bonnie bought the 200-acre farm and
  ran a "pick your own green beans" ad. The U-pick business grew (1974 → Farm Market), and
  **the restaurant opened in July 1983**, serving what Mary served in the 1920s–30s.
  Joe Huber III died in 2015; "Dream Big, Work Hard" carries on. Eight generations now live in Starlight.
- **Restaurant**: all-you-can-eat Country Platter, served family style (fried chicken +
  Huber honey ham, fried biscuits with apple butter, chicken & dumplings, mashed potatoes,
  green beans, slaw or seasonal salad). Plus country-style suppers, sandwiches, homemade
  pies and cobblers, Huber Orchard wines, Farmhouse vodka/rum/apple-whiskey cocktails,
  "dirty sodas". 18% gratuity for parties of 8+.
- **Joe's To Go**: carryout & online ordering (Clover). Family chicken dinners from 8 to 32
  pieces; group dinners for 50 / 100 people.
- **Hours (as published)**: Through December 2026, Thursday–Sunday 11 AM–7 PM, closed
  Monday–Wednesday (except Wed Nov 25). Thanksgiving: Nov 25 & 26, 11–7. Reopens in March —
  call for the date and holiday hours.
- **Barnyard**: free admission, same hours as the restaurant. 40-ft tunnel slide, Treetop
  Bus Stop, mini farm animals, fish & turtle feeding by the lake, covered lakeside pavilion.
- **Farm Market**: jarred goods, homemade fudge, baked goods, seasonal produce. Online shop at shop.joehubers.com.
- **Weddings**: gazebo by the lake and orchards, or indoor banquet halls. Perfect Match
  package (ceremony + reception, up to 100 guests, 5 hours, buffet, bartender, coordinator,
  arch, changing room, linens, centerpieces, bottle of Huber wine…). $1,000 non-refundable
  deposit. Event coordinator: Amy Huber Peters (amy@joehubers.com).
- **Special events**: company picnics (4-hour private facility, buffet, volleyball,
  basketball, fishing), birthday parties (pavilion $150), fall field trips (Sept–Oct,
  Mon–Fri, $15/child, $20/adult), Power Lunch (50+ guests, Mon–Thu).
  Director of Sales: L.J. Huber (LJ@joehubers.com).
- **Catering & delivery**: Country Platter buffet, chicken buffet and box lunches;
  delivery minimum 50 people on the website (40 in the PDF, so we publish the website figure).
  Thanksgiving turkey dinner in November. 18% service charge if staff stay to serve.

## What is wrong with the current site

1. **Template feel**: stock WordPress blocks, repeated "Click Here" tiles, duplicated headings.
2. **No hierarchy of intent**: the three things people come for (eat, celebrate, play
   on the farm) share equal weight with legal links. The menu is buried in a sub-nav.
3. **Weak storytelling**: an 1843 → today family story is hidden on one page.
4. **Conversion**: wedding/event inquiries go to a generic contact form with no event
   type, date or guest-count fields, though the copy asks for them.
5. **Media**: 66-image unlabelled gallery, no alt text anywhere, heavy uncompressed images.
6. **Mobile**: long stacked blocks, no persistent call/menu actions.
7. **SEO**: no structured data, no alt text, duplicate headings, menu items only in PDFs.

## Strategic direction

**"A family table in the Indiana knobs."** Lead with the feeling of arriving at the farm
for supper, then give each visitor intent a clear path:

| Intent | Primary path | Conversion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eat | Hero → Menu → Hours | View Menu · Order Online · Call to reserve |
| Celebrate | Weddings / Events | Structured inquiry (type, date, guests) |
| Visit with family | Farm & Barnyard | Directions, hours, Joe's To Go picnic |
| Take home | Market | shop.joehubers.com |

Design language: editorial heritage (Caslon, America's founding typeface, plus a
modern grotesque), warm dark espresso/charcoal grounds against cream paper, brass hairlines,
archival captions, grain. Photography is real but low-resolution, so the layouts use
framed and cropped compositions rather than stretching small images edge to edge.
No AI imagery, no stock footage. No 3D: no brand object would earn it, and it would cost
performance. No video: none is available.
